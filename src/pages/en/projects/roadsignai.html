RoadSignAI

A traffic-sign detector that takes dashcam video and draws every sign with its Spanish name, its code from Spain’s 2025 DGT catalogue and its confidence, tracking it across frames.

Problem

Detecting Spanish signs with their current codes has three difficulties. There is no complete, accessible Spanish dataset. The catalogue changed in 2025: “no overtaking”, for example, moved from R-302 to R-305. And signs are usually small objects, which hurts lightweight models.

I also wanted everything to fit on a 4 GB laptop GPU, with a network size chosen with a possible embedded deployment in mind. It is not a driver-assistance system: it is a perception project, and the README says so.

Approach

  1. DGT 2025 taxonomy36 classes
  2. Data mappingGTSDB / GTSRB → 2025 codes
  3. Architecturessingle vs two-stage
  4. Resolution640 vs 960 px
  5. TrackingByteTrack + overlay
  6. ExportONNX · FP16
  • Own taxonomy. 36 frequent, Vienna-compatible signs with their 2025 DGT code. It does not try to cover the whole catalogue.
  • Data. GTSDB for detection and GTSRB for crop classification, mapped to Spanish codes. I reviewed other sources and dropped them for a reason: the 25-class Spanish Roboflow dataset could not be found, and the Ceit-TSR repository returns 404.
  • No leakage between splits. GTSDB is split into contiguous blocks by file name (train 0–499, val 500–599, official test 600–899), never at random: two frames from the same stretch of road cannot end up one in train and one in test.
  • Fine-tuning, not training from scratch. It starts from pretrained YOLO detectors.
  • Two architectures. A single-stage 36-class detector, versus a generic “sign” detector followed by a MobileNetV3-Small classifier for the crop.

  • Capacity wins. Going from YOLO11n to YOLO11s adds +0.21 mAP50. Raising the resolution to 960 px helps less, although it does improve small boxes (0.331 → 0.407).
  • Frequent signs work very well. With YOLO11s: give way (R-1) 0.968, stop (R-2) 0.962, priority road (R-3) 0.949 and R-401 0.939.
  • Finding a sign is easy; reading the pictogram is not. A single-class “sign” detector reaches 0.974 mAP50, and the crop classifier scores 98.0% on the GTSRB test set. That is why the two-stage design reaches 0.951 recall.

What did not work

  • Rare signs. Infrequent warnings such as P-17 (0.067), P-22b (0.030) or R-506 (0.071) stay close to zero even with YOLO11s. With few examples, a bigger model cannot invent the missing ones.
  • The newest model was not the best. YOLO26n is slightly lighter, but on this data it does not beat YOLO11n (0.406 versus 0.417).
  • Distant signs at 640 px. They fail, as expected.
  • No embedded measurements. The network size was chosen with the edge in mind, but there are no tests on Jetson, Raspberry Pi or Hailo, and I do not claim any.

Next steps

  • Record Spanish roads to build an in-domain test set.
  • Retry Ceit-TSR as a hard validation set and add more classes.
  • Try INT8 only if it can be calibrated on this same GPU.
